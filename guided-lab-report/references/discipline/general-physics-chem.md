# General physics chemistry and yield analysis

Start from the objective, actual apparatus and conservation/constitutive equations. Confirm units, signs, reference conditions, replicate grouping and fit domain. Propose the smallest output set that answers the question.

## Conversion selectivity yield and closure

Define limiting reactant, reaction stoichiometry, reaction extent, desired product, and whether the basis is molar, mass, carbon or another conserved quantity. Report the chosen definitions beside the calculation.

- Conversion of A commonly uses `(A_in−A_out)/A_in` on a compatible molar basis. For batch work use the appropriate initial/final amounts; concentration alone is insufficient if volume changes.
- Desired-product yield can mean product relative to feed or relative to the theoretical product from consumed limiting reactant. Selectivity has multiple conventions. Use the assignment's definition and stoichiometric factors instead of imposing a single universal formula.
- Material-balance closure must include accumulation and the appropriate mass/element/component basis. Chemical reactions conserve total mass and elements, not necessarily total moles or each species. Do not call every inlet/outlet species difference an experimental error.

## Other experiments

Choose objective-specific plots and parameters: a physical relationship, calibration, response comparison or balance. Require a model before fitting, and state the fitting domain. For logs and reciprocal transforms, check their domain and units/reference scale.

Keep uncertainty sources distinct from percent disagreement. Do not insert significance tests, elaborate mechanism diagrams, sustainability claims or additional models unless needed for the agreed objectives.

## Coaching

Ask the student to identify the important relationship and propose a mechanism. Present numerical evidence and test their prediction through the relevant formula. For unrecognized experimental types, use the manual and verified method sources rather than extrapolating a discipline example beyond its assumptions.
